 Exaclim Dashboard Requirements
 Supervisor dashboard for the Exaclim worker heat dosimeter. Version 1, draft for team review.


 1. Purpose
 Exaclim warns workers early and personally about heat strain through a wristband (green, amber,
 red, with vibration). The dashboard is for supervisors. It helps them plan the work day around the heat
 and see how their crews are doing, without exposing any individual worker's health details.


 2. Users
 • Supervisor: the only dashboard user in this version. Plans the day, watches crew status,
     responds to help calls.


 3. What the dashboard shows
 The dashboard has three areas: the forecast, crew status, and suggested breaks. Nothing else.

 3.1 Heat forecast
 • D1. Show the heat risk for the site for today and tomorrow, hour by hour, using the public weather
     forecast for the site's location.
 • D2. Risk uses the same colors as the wristband: green, amber, red.
 • D3. Highlight high-risk windows clearly, for example "Tomorrow 11:00 to 15:00: high risk", so the
     supervisor can plan before the day starts.

 3.2 Crew status
 • D4. For each crew, show how many workers are currently green, amber and red.
 • D5. Show the crew's overall status as one color, taken from its most at-risk members.
 • D6. Show when the information was last updated.
 3.3 Suggested breaks and rotation
 • D7. For each crew, suggest when to take rest breaks or rotate workers into shade, based on the
     forecast and the crew's combined heat budget.
 • D8. Suggestions are advice. The supervisor decides.
 3.4 Help calls and emergencies
 • D9. When a worker presses the help button on the wristband or reports feeling unwell, show an
     emergency alert at the top of the dashboard immediately, with the name of the worker, the crew
     and the time.
 • D10. An emergency alert stays visible until a supervisor acknowledges it.
 3.5 Watches and setup
 • D11. Each crew has one shared site phone, kept by the supervisor or crew lead. It collects data
     from the watches by Bluetooth and sends it to the dashboard over the internet.
 • D12. The watch keeps working with no phone or signal. It calculates the heat budget and alerts
     the worker by itself, and the data reaches the dashboard when the connection returns.
 • D13. A help call made while the watch is out of range reaches the supervisor only once the watch
     reconnects. The watch still alerts the worker and people nearby.

 • D14. The supervisor creates crews, sets the site location and assigns watches to workers.
 • D15. Each worker's clothing type and heat acclimatization are set when the watch is issued, from
     a few simple options. Acclimatization increases automatically with the days the watch is worn, and
     can be changed. These details stay on the watch and are not shown on the dashboard.


 4. Privacy rules
 • P1. The dashboard shows crew-level information only. No individual worker's heat budget, history
     or health details appear on it. The only exception is a help call, which names the worker (D9).
 • P2. Crews of fewer than 3 workers are not shown on their own. They are grouped with another
     crew, so no individual stands out.
 • P3. Worker data is not used for discipline or unrelated employment decisions.

 5. Safety rules the dashboard follows
 • S1. A symptom report or help call always overrides everything else, including the forecast and
     crew status.
 • S2. Suggestions can make a warning earlier or more cautious, never weaker.
 • S3. If the forecast or crew data is missing or old, the dashboard says so and shows a cautious
     status. It never goes quiet or shows "all clear" by default.


 6. Usability
 • U1. A web app that supervisors log into from a phone or computer browser. One screen, readable
     outdoors, with large text and colors that are also distinguishable by label (not color alone).
 • U2. Works on a weak connection and shows clearly when it is out of date.
 • U3. A new supervisor can understand it without training.

 7. Open questions
 These need an answer from the team before the design is finished.
 1. Which watch model? It must send data over Bluetooth to a phone or web app and have a button
     for the help call.
 2. Which languages should the dashboard support first?

 8. Not in this version
 On-site sensors, individual worker views, reports and analytics, user accounts for roles other than
 supervisor, and any other alert channels such as SMS. They can be added later if the pilot shows
 they are needed.
